I built a parking task for a simulated SO-101 robot arm in MuJoCo. I collected 300 demonstrations with a script and used 280 to train the policy. In 20 development scenes, the model put two cars partly inside the correct bays.

## The simulator and the task

I started with one car and a straight push. An inverse kinematics controller moved the gripper behind the car. The gripper pushed the car. The wheels turned freely, and the car had no motor.

Then I added scenes with two to four cars and the same number of bays. Each instruction named a car and a bay by color. It told the arm to push the car or pick it up and place it. The car and bay did not have to match in color.

The arm used a raised mount and flat pads on the gripper. These changes helped it reach the bays and grip the cars. I changed where the cars and bays started within a fixed layout. The cars started in the same direction. The cameras and lights stayed fixed. The simulator used the SO-101 model from [MuJoCo Menagerie](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie).

## How I collected the data

I used a script to make the demonstrations. It read the car and bay positions from MuJoCo and converted points along the arm path into joint commands. Cars moved only when the arm pushed or gripped them.

I accepted a demonstration when the correct car stopped upright and fully inside its bay. The car had to be on the table and free of the gripper. I rejected the demonstration if another car moved more than 5 mm. A pickup required gripper contact on both sides of the car and a lift of at least 25 mm.

The first collection had 100 accepted demonstrations. I used 80 for training and kept 20 for development. I later added 200 training demonstrations. The final training set had 140 pushes and 140 pick-and-place demonstrations. The 20 development scenes stayed the same.

I replayed the recorded commands before I exported the data to LeRobot. Each row pairs an instruction, two camera images, and six joint positions with the six targets for the next command. I used only the training data to calculate how to scale the joint positions and targets.

## How I trained the policy

I trained SmolVLA first, for 5,000 updates on 80 demonstrations. It parked none of the 20 cars in the development scenes. I checked the camera order, joint order, units, and timestamps. I found no clear error in those checks.

Next I trained from the base LeRobot π₀.₅ checkpoint with 280 demonstrations. I changed the model and the dataset in the same run. That run gives one result for both changes. [LeRobot π₀.₅ documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/pi05).

I kept the vision and language weights fixed. I trained the action expert, which predicts how the joints move, and the layers that pass action and time data to it. I used supervised flow matching to teach the model to turn noise into joint targets based on the observations and instruction.

The controller sent 20 commands per second. The model predicted 50 commands at a time. The controller used the first 10, then read new camera images and joint positions. The model never received car or bay coordinates. The evaluation code used those coordinates to score results and stop unsafe attempts.

## What the tests measured

A full park required the entire car to be inside the bay. The car also had to stay upright and free of the gripper for at least 0.5 seconds after it came to rest. I counted a separate partial placement when the car overlapped the correct bay after release and passed the safety checks.

Training reached 7,000 updates. I compared the checkpoints on six development scenes and selected the checkpoint at 3,000 updates. Those six scenes were part of the 20-scene evaluation. At that checkpoint, two cars ended on the table with part of the car inside the correct bay. Neither car fit fully inside its bay.

The validation loss decreased from 0.0138 at 3,000 updates to 0.0108 at 4,000. In one recorded case, the 4,000-update policy still lifted the car but released it farther from the bay. The loss measures prediction error on recorded data. I checked the final car position in the simulator to measure how well the policy parked.

I tried the same checkpoint on four new layouts and changed the inference settings. The policy made zero full parks and zero safe partial placements. I changed both factors, so the test gives no separate result for either one.

## Two follow-up experiments

### Train more of the model

I trained a second π₀.₅ policy from the same generic base. This run also updated the vision layers and added LoRA adapters to the language and action layers. I compared both 3,000-update policies on the same 20 scenes, with the same inference settings and noise samples.

Both policies scored zero full parks and zero bay overlaps in that comparison. The second policy made one safe pickup in ten pick-and-place scenes. It carried the car over the bay and released it outside. The first policy made zero safe pickups in this comparison.

### Add corrections to the data

I recorded attempts from new starting positions. A script took control partway through each attempt and finished the task. I kept 20 runs and used only the part after the script took control. In 19 of those runs, the gripper did not hold a car when the script took control.

I started two runs from the same checkpoint and gave each 500 more updates. One run used the original demonstrations. In the other run, 25% of the rows came from the corrections. Every correction came from a pick-and-place task, so that run used more examples of that action.

I tested each run on 20 development scenes and 20 new scenes. Both scored zero full parks. In the new scenes, adding corrections cut the share of control steps with an out-of-range command from about 49% to 23%. That drop did not change the final parking result.

## What I plan to try next

I plan to start with a car already in the gripper and test whether the model can lower and release it inside the bay. Then I want to try two changes to the training data.

- **Collect more examples of carrying and release.** Only one of the 20 earlier corrections started with a car in the gripper. I plan to collect more demonstrations from that state, at different distances and angles to the bay. I will compare two runs from the same checkpoint, with the same number of updates. One will use the original data. The other will include the new examples.
- **Train on simpler scenes first.** I plan to train a pick-and-place policy with one car and one bay. Once it can park the car on new layouts, I will add other cars and vary the starting angles. I want to see when the extra objects or angles start to cause failures.

I will keep the evaluation layouts out of the training data. I will count full parks first and record where each failed run stops. That will show whether a change helps with a grasp, the move to the bay, or the release.

The results above apply to the simulator and its layouts. The repository includes the simulator, teacher, training code, and evaluation tools. The public checkpoint is the first π₀.₅ policy at 3,000 updates.
